**Working title:** Smartphone Use During Lectures Among Undergraduate Information Systems Students at Addis Ababa University

## 1. Background of the Study

Smartphones are now part of daily life for university students. Students carry them everywhere, including to lectures. A smartphone can be useful in class. A student can look up a word, take a photo of the board, or open the lecture notes. It can also be used for things that have nothing to do with the lecture, such as chatting, social media, and games.

Some studies have looked at phone use in class. Kuznekoff and Titsworth (2013) found that students who used their phones during a lecture took fewer notes and scored lower on a test about that lecture. Kates et al. (2018) reviewed many studies and reported that more phone use goes together with lower grades.

These studies were done at other universities, and students' habits can be different from one university to another. This study is important because it will give the department facts about how its own students use smartphones during lectures. Lecturers can use these facts when they decide on rules for phone use in class.

## 2. Problem Statement

Many lecturers see students using their phones during lectures. Research shows that phone use in class is linked with lower learning and lower grades (Kates et al., 2018; Kuznekoff & Titsworth, 2013). However, there is no available data on how often Information Systems students at AAU use their smartphones during lectures, or what they use them for.

Without this data, lecturers can only guess. The problem this study addresses is the lack of recorded data on how often these students use smartphones during lectures and for which purposes.

## 3. Research Questions

- **RQ1:** How often do undergraduate Information Systems students at AAU use their smartphones during lectures?
- **RQ2:** For what purposes do these students use their smartphones during lectures?

## 4. Research Objectives

**General objective:** To investigate how undergraduate Information Systems students at AAU use their smartphones during lectures.

**Specific objectives:**
1. To determine how often students use their smartphones during lectures.
2. To identify the purposes for which students use their smartphones during lectures.

## 5. Proposed Research Method

- **Research approach:** Quantitative, descriptive survey.
- **Data source(s):** Primary data from undergraduate Information Systems students at AAU. The sample is about 100 students from different year levels.
- **Data collection method(s):** A questionnaire with closed-ended questions. Frequency is measured with a scale (never, rarely, sometimes, often, always). Purposes are measured with a list of options that students tick. The questionnaire is shared online (for example, Google Forms).
- **Data analysis method(s):** Descriptive statistics (frequencies and percentages), shown in tables and charts, using Microsoft Excel.
